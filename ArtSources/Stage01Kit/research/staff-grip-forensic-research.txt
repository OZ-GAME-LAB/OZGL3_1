Staff grip forensic research — 2026-10-21

Status and correction to the earlier review

The previous orb review verified the head silhouette, floating gap, face clearance, rendering, and preserved shaft geometry. It did not verify that the hand physically enclosed the shaft. Treating that review as approval of the grip was insufficient. A socket or marker matching the hand bone proves a transform relationship, not palm/finger/thumb contact. No corrective asset, clip, or game code has been edited during this investigation. The only new code is the explicitly authorized read-only ChibiGripProbe.cs diagnostic; the root agent owns its Unity execution.

5. Actual shaft diameter requires triangle slicing, not filtering nearby vertices:
   The shaft has long triangles and no vertices inside original axial Y[-.12,+.12] m. Selecting vertices around the grip therefore gives an empty set. Intersect the triangles with original shaft Y=0 (also checked Y=-.04,+.04). The 24 resulting cross-section points have X[-.0240877,+.0240877], Z[-.0219303,+.0262452] m. At review scale .70, maximum radial extent is .0188631 m and radial95=.0188145 m: approximately 37.7 mm effective diameter. Comparing only a hand-bone marker or an assumed thin cylinder would miss this geometry.

6. Changing only the mount direction is not yet a validated fix:
   The initial physically sensible axis candidate follows the finger columns, approximately hand Z, with an oblique fit allowed. However, actual Grip75 triangles sliced at hand Z=-.035,-.020,0,+.020 do not establish a free 18.86 mm-radius channel. For example, at tentative XY=(-.025,.090) m the nearest section-surface distances are approximately 4.49,3.92,10.42,3.06 mm. These are unsigned distances; outside/inside and thumb-web intersections still need classification. They show why a guessed new offset must not be called a successful grip. The generic 75 morph was not authored around this staff's actual cross-section; a dedicated StaffGrip corrective may be needed.

7. Skin-weight drift is a separate issue:
   The original right thumb is only about .72..845 hand-weighted in its selected region; the forearm contributes to its skinning. A wrist rotation can therefore change the thumb's shape in hand-local space even when Grip_R stays 75. Strongly hand-weighted finger vertices are more rigid relative to the hand. Fitting only the bind-space morph can appear correct in one frame and lose contact at the cast extreme. The actual saved clip must be sampled and CPU-skinned at ready, preparation, execution, recovery, and end. No claim about live skinning is based on the bind-space measurements above.

Exact read-only probe and calculations

Root Unity method: ChibiGripProbe.Run(). Outputs ArtSources/Stage01Kit/research/StaffGripProbe. It samples the actual v007 review prefab with saved Idle_Fists0, Staff_Cast_Forward0, and Staff_Cast_Forward.24s. Each pose is BakeMesh'd at Grip_R 0,75,100 on disposable instances. It exports source vertex IDs, per-bone weights, source bind-hand positions, live deformed normals/vertices, remapped hand triangles, all current staff mesh geometry under the exact ReviewProp transform, and XY/YZ/XZ orthographic closeups with orange diagnostic shaft. No SaveAssets, SavePrefab, importer, or clip mutation is present.

World skinning is conceptually p_world=sum_i(weight_i * boneWorld_i * bindpose_i * blendedMeshVertex). Use the actual BakeMesh result to include Unity's blendshape and skinning semantics. Convert a baked vertex to physical hand metres with p_hand = inverse(hand.worldRotation) * (renderer.TransformPoint(bakedVertex)-hand.worldPosition). A bone InverseTransformPoint alone can return scaled bone units; explicitly check lossyScale and units. Probe snapshots use the orthonormal world-rotation definition. For normals use the corresponding world normal transformed by inverse hand rotation; do not infer the palm's anatomical normal from a global sign.

Recommended calibration, to be implemented only after the probe is inspected:

* Determine the palm support patch, four curled finger pad regions, thumb pad, and thumb web from actual mesh/texture and source vertex IDs. The region masks above are starting proxies. Inspect all three orthographic views and at least one oblique view. Fit a local palm patch using normals/PCA; identify the opposition direction from the actual curl and thumb surface.
* Slice the actual shaft into the exact cross-section at the intended axial grip span. Fit a cylinder or the true faceted cross-section through the empty channel across multiple finger columns. Do not minimise distance to all hand points indiscriminately, which can select a skin-intersecting or exterior-air solution. Check actual triangle intersections and radial first-hit distances around the channel, allowing only a small contact tolerance. A hollow gap, deep shaft burial in the palm, or thumb on the same side as the fingers is a failure even if some nearest distance is zero.
* Let fitted hand-space channel centre be c and axis a. With original weapon grip pivot g, object scale s and rotation Q mapping weapon +Y to a, the physical hand-space root position is c-Q*(s*g). Here g is currently zero. The offset and rotation must be derived together. Roll should preserve the staff hardware's intended presentation. Do not reuse (.040,.025,0) or assume an unchanged Z90 rotation.
* The existing HandFrame maps hand -X to world blade direction. A staff channel approximately along hand Z needs its own hand-to-staff basis; otherwise rotating the mount changes the cast direction or forces an implausible wrist. Check the wrist and forearm silhouette under the calibrated physical grip. This is preview/baked animation calibration, not a request for runtime IK code.
* If no adequate channel exists at the actual 37.7 mm shaft diameter, author an object-specific grip corrective around the measured shaft surface. Keep the existing full fist and other prop grips separate. Confirm fingers wrap, the thumb opposes them, and pads lie on the shaft surface; don't shrink/move the rod merely to make a marker pass. Recheck thumb drift between the identical probe vertices at Staff0 and Staff.24s before deciding whether mount adjustment alone is enough.
* Re-render native side/2.5D plus closeups for the entire cast, including the stable end. Test transitions with the same attached prop. Validate surface contact, wrist shape, continuous attachment and cast direction separately. Numerical alignment alone is not a naturalness score.

Read official page. It describes transform following and retention of initial position/rotation offsets; it does not make the child conform to a hand mesh. Relevance: following a bone with a maintained offset does not establish a physically valid grip. A calibrated object-space relationship is required before attachment.

https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.3/animation/constraints/relationship/child_of.html
Read indexed official documentation; direct re-open failed. Set Inverse preserves an already posed object's relationship when the parent constraint is assigned. Relevance: first pose the hand/weapon relationship correctly, then preserve it; parenting is not a shape-fitting operation. Do not attribute anatomical grip instructions to this source.

Research conclusion

The primary workflows agree on a specific object-fitting hand pose plus a correctly calibrated attach transform. Our local failures are concrete: mismatched shaft/channel axis, wrist-side generic offset, a generic morph not fitted to the staff diameter, and potential partial-thumb-weight drift during wrist motion. Fixing these requires actual live geometry and closeup review. Additional AI body animation generation would not, by itself, correct this hand-to-prop relationship.

First candidate was rejected after actual inspection

The first axisZ candidate enclosed the shaft more than the baseline, but actual Staff hand detail and calibrated XY/YZ/XZ images showed thin plate-like finger surfaces, a flattened thumb crossing and a needle-shaped web edge. Its reported zero profile intrusion did not make it visually acceptable. Identical source edges5292-5294 and1119-1122 grew from about6.08/5.44 mm to26.23/23.04 mm. The generic per-column X envelope included longitudinal fan drift, compressing a local finger section into too little radial thickness. Live source(Y,Z) ray sections showed median X thickness41.38 mm across222 usable distal-finger samples, versus full-column envelopes up to76.32 mm. In addition, independently projected UV-seam duplicates could diverge during triangle corrections, producing cracks and spikes.

The subsequent candidate changes ONLY Grip_R150, keeping the original75/100 frame deltas and the prior weight correction fixed. It measures each source vertex's actual(Y,Z) triangle-ray X span; it welds identical source positions for correction and regularizes edge stretch. The shaft/palm relationship is now diagonal: mount rotationX45, axis(0,.707107,.707107), radialY(0,.707107,-.707107), with the same physical centre and scale. Shape deformation and collision checks use that basis. The 45-degree value is a character-specific candidate design, not an angle prescribed by a reference. A perpendicular rod/palm basis combined with the desired forward aim had forced excessive wrist bend in the first candidate, so hand shape and forearm orientation must be evaluated together. Native closeups and a new probe are required before this second candidate can be judged.
